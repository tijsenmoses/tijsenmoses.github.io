Webcam / Intercom Retrofit

Cellerate Power - Engineering & Operations Associate, Summer 2026

  • SolidWorks
  • 3D printing
  • Design for constraints
  • Field installation
  • EV charging
What
Designed and fabricated a retrofit for a webcam/intercom customer support and security system to be installed on top of Cellerate Power’s two C7 EV chargers, without drilling into the units, which would void their warranty.
How
Took measurements at the site, and on possible webcam mounting locations. Decided that the best location to mount the webcams was on the foldable handle at the top of the chargers. The mounting hardware would have to be easily removable in case technicians need to use the handle to open the top of the unit. I chose a two-piece mount that clamps the handle together using screws pulling the two pieces together. The parts were designed in DS SolidWorks and manufactured on a 3D printer, which required four iterations of prototypes because of the handle’s irregular curved geometry. To power the cameras, I had to design and install two conduits (one for each charger) with low voltage power to each unit, with an electrician making the final power connections.
Results
The system lets Cellerate see the charging area and talk to customers in real time, providing near-instant support when a session fails. Customers leaving after a failed charge dropped by roughly half, and customers have given strong positive feedback on the way they now receive help.

Salt Distribution System

Group project - Introduction to Engineering Design, RPI

  • Fluid mechanics
  • Fusion 360
  • 3D printing
  • Electromechanical integration
  • Team of five

Five-person team in Intro to Engineering Design designed and fabricated a working prototype that spreads salt evenly on walkways using a high-velocity air stream, with the salt, controls and air source carried in a backpack. Each subsystem was led by a student. I led the Air System and Valve subsystems and designed 14+ custom components.

Air System

What
Designed and built a handheld nozzle and tubing system that carries salt in the airflow and deposits it without blowing it away, with a button on the handle controlling the whole device.
How
Used the continuity equation to size the nozzle exit so air slows from the blower rated airspeed of 150 mph in the tube to about 16 mph at the exit, slow enough that salt settles instead of blowing away. Autodesk Fusion 360 and 3D printing were used to design and manufacture the parts.
V1A1 = V2A2  →  V2 = V1 πr12πr22 = 150 mph × (32.5 mm)2(100 mm)2 ≈ 15.8 mph Tube radius r1 = 32.5 mm, nozzle exit radius r2 = 100 mm, V1 = blower rated airspeed.
Results
Salt was carried reliably and landed where the nozzle was aimed, without blowing away salt already on the ground.

Valve

What
Designed and fabricated hardware for a valve that when integrated with electronic components, can control the release of salt into the airflow system.
How
A pinch valve type was chosen. Autodesk Fusion 360 was used to design parts that would be manufactured on 3D printers. The parts were designed together with the lead of the motor controls, to allow the motor and controls to be mounted to the valve.
Results
The valve fully sealed when closed and was able to block the flow of salt into the air system, until prompted by a button, which opened the valve and allowed salt to pass through.

Model Chairlift

Personal project

  • Fusion 360
  • 250+ printed parts
  • Tolerancing
  • Motorized drive
What
Designed and built a 1/32 scale model of a Leitner-Poma "LPA" chairlift.
How
Used Autodesk Fusion 360 to design dozens of parts and piece them together in an assembly. The drive terminal side was designed with mounting hardware for a motor and wiring. Applied 3D printer tolerance specifications to designs to ensure proper assembly. 3D printed each part, totaling over 250 parts. Utilized plastic welding and hundreds of M1 machine screws for assembly.
Results
A working motorized model chairlift. Next step: the mounting needs to be made more structurally stable, because the lift can shift during operation, which misaligns it and lets the rope come off the sheaves.
